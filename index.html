About

I will begin a PhD in Engineering & Public Policy at Carnegie Mellon in August 2026, working on the governance of artificial intelligence and emerging digital technologies — the institutions, rules, and norms that shape how these systems are built and deployed.

Most recently I worked at Ernst & Young in Luxembourg (September 2023–May 2026), joining as a Junior Consultant in Business Transformation and promoted to Senior Consultant in June 2025. I advised public-sector and corporate clients on technology-driven change: leading a project with a major European institution to assess the digital maturity of Luxembourg's public services online, piloting a human–AI workflow for regulatory news monitoring, reviewing the digital presence of an engineering firm against responsible-design principles, conducting a market study of Luxembourg's transportation systems, and supporting Luxembourg's Ministry of Defense on recruitment-process optimisation.

Before EY I read for an MPhil in Technology Policy at the University of Cambridge, and a BSc in International Management at King's College London — with an exchange year at the Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania, and a Global Business term at Tecnológico de Monterrey in Mexico. During and around the MPhil I worked as a visiting policy researcher at the British Office of Communications (Ofcom) on the safety of generative AI for children, and as a visiting researcher at the European Commission on the intersection of IoT, AI, and the circular economy. Earlier, I was a research assistant in the Cambridge Experimental and Behavioural Economics Group on generative AI and creativity, a Science & Technology research intern with the Think Tank and Civil Societies Program at the Lauder Institute (University of Pennsylvania), and a Business Development intern at IBM in Dublin.

My broader interest is in how regulatory frameworks can be made both technically literate and democratically legitimate as AI systems become more capable and more consequential. I work in French, English, Polish, and Luxembourgish.

Research

My work sits at the intersection of public policy, law, and the management of technology. Three threads run through it.

Human–AI collaboration in organisations. How digital technologies — and generative AI in particular — are reshaping work, communication, creativity, and productivity, and what good collaboration between humans and machines looks like in practice.

Ethical technology in business. How organisations can design, deploy, and govern user-centred technologies responsibly, balancing commercial pressure with accountability to the people their products affect.

Societal impacts of digital technologies. Implications for privacy, digital governance, and the wellbeing of children online — with attention to comparative regulatory approaches across the EU, US, and UK.

Methodologically I work primarily in literature review and qualitative research (semi-structured interviews, document analysis), with foundational skills in quantitative methods (R, Python, SPSS) and survey design (Qualtrics, Microsoft Forms).
