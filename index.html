About

I am an incoming PhD student in Engineering & Public Policy at Carnegie Mellon, where I study how societies govern artificial intelligence and other emerging digital technologies — the institutions, rules, and norms that shape how these systems are built and deployed.

Before Carnegie Mellon, I read for an MPhil in Technology Policy at Cambridge and an undergraduate degree in International Management at King's College London, with a year at the Wharton School. I then spent three years at Ernst & Young as a business transformation consultant, working with large organisations on technology-driven change.

My broader interest is in how regulatory frameworks can be made both technically literate and democratically legitimate, particularly as AI systems become more capable and more consequential.

Research

My work sits between law, public policy, and computer science, asking how governance frameworks can keep pace with technologies that move faster than the institutions meant to regulate them. I draw on political economy, legal analysis, and science & technology studies.

I am especially interested in comparative approaches to AI regulation — why the EU, US, and UK have taken such different paths, and what those choices imply for both innovation and the public interest.
