Introduction

About Me

I am an incoming PhD student in Engineering and Public Policy at Carnegie Mellon University. My research focuses on how societies govern artificial intelligence and emerging digital technologies — examining the institutions, rules, and norms shaping their development and deployment.

I completed an MPhil in Technology Policy at the University of Cambridge and studied International Management at King's College London, with a year abroad at the Wharton School, University of Pennsylvania. I then spent three years as a Business Transformation Consultant at Ernst & Young, advising large organisations on technology-driven change.

I am broadly interested in how regulatory frameworks can be designed to be both technically informed and democratically legitimate — especially as AI systems become more capable and more consequential.

Research Interests

My work sits at the intersection of law, policy, and computer science — examining how governance frameworks can keep pace with rapidly evolving technologies. I draw on political economy, legal analysis, and science & technology studies.

I am particularly interested in comparative approaches to AI regulation: understanding why different jurisdictions (EU, US, UK) have chosen distinct strategies and what the implications are for innovation and societal outcomes.
